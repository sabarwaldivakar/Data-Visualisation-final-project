1. Business context¶

Olist connects small businesses with Brazilian marketplace customers. Its anonymised dataset records real orders and reviews from 2016–2018 (Olist, 2021).

Audience (Who): Operations and customer-experience managers.¶

Problem (What): With limited capacity, which periods, regions and delivery stages deserve attention first?¶

Approach (How): I connect delivery reliability with poor reviews and narrow the investigation. I define a poor review as 1–2 stars. The analysis prioritises further investigation; it does not prove causes or forecast savings.¶

2. Dataset and approach¶

I use the Olist dataset (Olist, 2021), licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The analysis uses orders, customers, reviews, items, products and category translations. Payments, sellers and geolocation are outside this question.

The unit of analysis is one order. Item and review tables can contain several rows per order. customer_id links location but does not identify repeat customers. Run this notebook from the project folder with the original CSV files inside data/.

4. Preparing one row per order¶

I convert dates and use checked joins. Late means delivery after the promised calendar day. I retain delivered orders with non-negative elapsed time and a positive promised duration. Unreviewed orders are excluded only from review-rate denominators.

date_columns = ['order_purchase_timestamp', 'order_delivered_carrier_date', 'order_delivered_customer_date',
                'order_estimated_delivery_date']
for column in date_columns:
    orders[column] = pd.to_datetime(orders[column], errors='coerce')
reviews['review_answer_timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(reviews['review_answer_timestamp'], errors='coerce')

def elapsed_days(end, start):
    return (end - start).dt.total_seconds() / 86400

latest_review = reviews.sort_values('review_answer_timestamp').drop_duplicates('order_id', keep='last')
df = orders.merge(customers[['customer_id', 'customer_state']],
                  on='customer_id', how='left', validate='one_to_one')
df = df.merge(latest_review[['order_id', 'review_score', 'review_answer_timestamp']],
              on='order_id', how='left', validate='one_to_one')
df['delivery_days'] = elapsed_days(df['order_delivered_customer_date'], df['order_purchase_timestamp'])
df['promise_days'] = elapsed_days(df['order_estimated_delivery_date'], df['order_purchase_timestamp'])
df['late'] = df['order_delivered_customer_date'].dt.date > df['order_estimated_delivery_date'].dt.date
valid = (df['order_status'] == 'delivered') & df['delivery_days'].ge(0) & df['promise_days'].gt(0)
delivered = df.loc[valid].copy()
rated = delivered.dropna(subset=['review_score']).copy()
rated['poor_review'] = rated['review_score'].le(2)
print('All orders:', len(df), '| Valid delivered:', len(delivered), '| Rated:', len(rated))
print('Purchase range:', orders['order_purchase_timestamp'].min(), 'to', orders['order_purchase_timestamp'].max())
print('Unmatched customer states:', df['customer_state'].isna().sum())

5. Five insights¶

This means poor reviews are proportionally much more common among late orders; it does not mean that most poor reviews come from late orders.

However, 4,653 responses precede recorded delivery. Olist invites reviews after delivery or when the promised date is due (Olist, 2021). Among responses submitted after receipt, the rates are 19.4% versus 9.3% (1,908 late and 89,263 on-time orders). This filter creates a different and much smaller late-order sample, so neither comparison proves how much of the difference was caused by lateness.

Furthermore, 67.5% of all poor reviews concern on-time orders (8,289 of 12,272) because the on-time group is much larger. Delivery reliability and late-order communication deserve investigation, but fixing lateness alone will not resolve every poor review.

5.5 Late orders spend more delivery time after carrier handoff¶

Decision: At which recorded stage do delays concentrate? I split purchase-to-delivery duration at carrier handoff, validate the chronological order of the timestamps and divide both stages by each order's total delivery duration. A box plot with delivery-status hue compares the distributions.

Before handoff covers purchase to recorded carrier handoff, including order approval, seller preparation and waiting for collection. After handoff covers carrier handoff to customer delivery, including transport and last-mile delivery.

delivered['before_days'] = elapsed_days(delivered['order_delivered_carrier_date'],
                                        delivered['order_purchase_timestamp'])
delivered['after_days'] = elapsed_days(delivered['order_delivered_customer_date'],
                                       delivered['order_delivered_carrier_date'])
stages = delivered.loc[delivered['before_days'].ge(0) & delivered['after_days'].ge(0) &
                       delivered['delivery_days'].gt(0)].copy()
stages['Before handoff'] = 100 * stages['before_days'] / stages['delivery_days']
stages['After handoff'] = 100 * stages['after_days'] / stages['delivery_days']
stages['Delivery status'] = np.where(stages['late'], 'Late', 'On time')
stage_long = stages.melt(id_vars='Delivery status', value_vars=['Before handoff', 'After handoff'],
                         var_name='Stage', value_name='Share of delivery time (%)')
g = seaborn.catplot(data=stage_long, x='Stage', y='Share of delivery time (%)',
    hue='Delivery status', hue_order=['On time', 'Late'], kind='box',
    palette=[light_red, dark_red], showfliers=False, height=4.5, aspect=1.6, legend_out=False)
g.set(ylim=(0, 100))
plt.title('Median post-handoff share reaches 89% for late orders', loc='left', pad=16)

6. Recommendations and critical evaluation¶

  1. Operations: investigate RJ journeys across major categories with February–March incident records. Volume, rates and cross-category consistency support examining dispatch, handoff and delivery events.
  2. Customer experience: inspect post-handoff delays and late-order communication. Track late-delivery and poor-review percentages separately with consistent denominators and review timing. The 53.2-point gap is not a causal effect.
  3. Investigate on-time complaints in parallel, particularly in high-volume SP. On-time orders account for 67.5% of poor reviews. Read comments before attributing complaints to products, sellers or delivery; this notebook analyses scores rather than classifying text.

Strengths: Checked order-level joins prevent repeated items/reviews from increasing an order's weight. The analysis states denominators, reports samples, applies selective cleaning and checks review timing.

Limitations: Completed/reviewed-order filters omit unresolved and unreviewed experiences, and some reviews precede delivery. Category/state selection excludes smaller groups. Seller behaviour, product mix and time may influence comparisons; no uncertainty intervals or multivariable adjustment are used. Historical Brazilian data cannot justify current budgets, carrier rankings or savings. Current operational records are needed before testing an intervention.

Conclusion¶

Begin with RJ service journeys and post-handoff events while reviewing on-time complaints separately. These findings identify historical investigation priorities; they do not prove why individual orders were delayed or poorly reviewed.
